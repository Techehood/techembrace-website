Case Study · Croydon Professional Women — Executive AI Briefing

From ungoverned adoption to a framework leaders can run themselves.

Challenge

Across the room, AI adoption had already happened — just with no one governing it. Attendees were already using free public AI tools for real business work, held back only by the same three questions Techembrace's own Five AI Personas research keeps finding behind AI hesitancy: “Will I get in trouble?”, “Will this give me my Friday back?”, and “Can I verify this without looking stupid?”

Solution

Daniel delivered “The AI Executive Blueprint” — a plain-English executive briefing built on Pillar 05’s GAT‑M governance framework (Govern, Assess, Train, Monitor) and Five AI Personas diagnostic, alongside co-speaker Helen Colechin on AI in HR. The session reframed AI adoption around the “Vault vs. Public Park” data-security model, and made the case — drawing on Pillar 03’s neurodiversity-first design work — for AI as a cognitive prosthetic that removes workflow friction for ADHD and dyslexic staff specifically.

Result

Daniel was the first male speaker the group had invited to any of its meetings — a trust signal in a room that doesn’t hand out platforms lightly. The session sparked direct follow-up interest in named tools, including NotebookLM, and left attendees with a governance framework built for business leaders, not just IT teams.

The GAT‑M Framework, Live

GAT‑M is Techembrace’s answer to “AI is a business utility now — manage it with intention.” Govern sets acceptable use and data boundaries with tiered risk levels, to shut down Shadow AI before it starts. Assess maps specific tools against real risk appetite rather than banning AI outright. Train builds prompt literacy and critical thinking so staff stay in control of the output, not the other way round. Monitor keeps a human verification loop on every output that leaves the building — a documented human-in-the-loop is treated as non-negotiable, not optional.

The same briefing made the strategic case for neurodiversity: AI as a “cognitive prosthetic” that turns an overwhelming report into manageable micro-tasks, removing the blank-page inertia that costs ADHD and dyslexic staff the most cognitive energy — a live application of the inclusive design principle behind Pillar 03’s own curriculum work.

Service Pillars

This project draws on Pillar 05 — Digital Transformation & Staff Enablement (GAT‑M governance, the Five AI Personas diagnostic) and Pillar 03 — Instructional Design & Digital Curriculum (neurodiversity-first design).
